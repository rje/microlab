# 2026 architecture review for the coding-specialist design

Deep-research sweep (108 agents, 5 search angles, 3-vote adversarial verification per claim),
run 2026-07-29 as the recipe-vintage gate before freezing the frontier design.

## Summary

The verified 2025–2026 literature strongly endorses the lab's existing hybrid-linear-attention plan — the Gated DeltaNet lineage has direct peer-reviewed 1.3B-scale wins, and Kimi Linear confirms that the planned ~3:1 KDA:MLA layerwise hybrid composes linear attention WITH MLA rather than competing with it (though its quality claims are only validated at 48B-A3B by the originating lab). The strongest verified additions NOT on the existing list, ranked for local ablation lanes, are: (1) Peri-LN normalization — beats Pre-LN at 400M–3.2B and more than halves seed-to-seed variance, which directly de-noises small ablations; (2) MoBA block-sparse attention — loss parity within ~1e-3 of full attention validated at exactly 568M–2.1B and implementable in plain PyTorch; (3) ASA-style layer alternation of local (sliding-window) and global (compressed/selective) attention — matches or beats both full attention and NSA at 340M/1.3B, the best sub-2B sparse-attention evidence found; (4) mHC-constrained hyper-connections — ~0.03 loss reduction at OLMo-1B, but only with DeepSeek's doubly-stochastic manifold constraint and with a material +26% training-memory cost. Two patterns should be avoided or deferred at this scale: multi-token prediction (explicitly worse than next-token at 300M–1.3B, gains only at 3B+ even on code) and raw NSA (validated only at 27B-MoE with headline speedups that are 64k-context phenomena). Nothing on the existing list is refuted, but Gated DeltaNet-2 (May 2026) reportedly supersedes both GDN and KDA under the same 1.3B protocol and deserves tracking, and the verification pipeline produced no surviving evidence on the WSD-vs-cosine, nanoGPT-speedrun, or code-recipe (tokenizer/data-mix/FIM-rate) legs of the question, which remain open.

## Verified findings

### 1. (confidence: high; votes: 3-0, 3-0, 2-1 (three merged claims))

The lab's planned ~3:1 linear:full hybrid is exactly the released Kimi Linear architecture: a layerwise stack of three Kimi Delta Attention (KDA) layers per one global MLA layer, where KDA extends Gated DeltaNet with finer-grained gating via a specialized Diagonal-Plus-Low-Rank transition-matrix variant that is cheaper than general DPLR. Moonshot claims it outperforms full-attention MLA under an identical recipe across short-context, long-context, and RL regimes — but that quality claim is validated only at 48B-total/3B-active MoE (fair comparisons at 1.4T tokens) and is the originating lab's own; there is no ≤2B dense replication. Verdict: proceed with the planned KDA+MLA evaluation (the structural design is confirmed as current best practice), but treat quality parity as something to verify locally, not assume.

**Evidence:** Abstract (verbatim): 'with an identical training recipe, Kimi Linear outperforms full MLA with a sizeable margin across all evaluated tasks.' Official README/model card: 'A 3:1 KDA-to-global MLA ratio reduces memory usage while maintaining or surpassing the quality of full attention.' KDA 'extends Gated DeltaNet with a finer-grained gating mechanism' and its chunkwise algorithm 'substantially reduces computation compared to the general DPLR formulation.' Verifiers confirmed the full benchmark validation exists only at 48B-A3B (scaling-law runs at 653M–1.7B activated are MoE loss-curves only, ~1.16x compute efficiency vs MLA); MLA layers in the hybrid use NoPE with positional modeling delegated to KDA — also matching the lab's NoPE plan. A July 2026 third-party fact-check grades 48B parity 'verified' but notes minimal independent replication and the MiniMax M2 counterexample where dev-scale linear-hybrid parity failed at frontier scale.

**Sources:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26692 | https://github.com/MoonshotAI/Kimi-Linear | https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-Linear-48B-A3B-Instruct

### 2. (confidence: high; votes: 3-0, 3-0, 3-0 (three merged claims))

The Gated DeltaNet lineage — the direct ancestor of KDA — has the best evidence at exactly the lab's target scale: peer-reviewed (ICLR 2025) head-to-head runs at 1.3B params/100B FineWeb-Edu tokens (ablations at 400M/15B) show GDN beating Mamba2 and DeltaNet on language modeling and commonsense reasoning, large in-context-retrieval gains (S-NIAH-2 at 4K: 92.2 vs Mamba2's 56.2), lowest RNN-model perplexity extrapolating to 20K — and, critically for the hybrid plan, GDN+sliding-window-attention hybrids beat pure GDN (H1 avg 56.40, H2 56.18 vs 55.32 pure). Verdict: this validates the hybrid-linear lane already on the list; note the hybrid evidence uses SWA rather than full global attention, so 3:1 linear:full is supported by close analogy, and NVIDIA's Gated DeltaNet-2 (arXiv 2605.22791, May 2026) reran the same protocol and reportedly beats GDN, KDA, and Mamba-3 — track it as a possible drop-in upgrade.

**Evidence:** Paper (verbatim): 'all models are trained under identical conditions with 1.3B parameters on 100B tokens sampled from the FineWeb-Edu dataset.' Table 2: GDN WikiText ppl 16.42 / avg commonsense 55.32 vs Mamba2 16.56/54.89 and DeltaNet 17.71/52.14. S-NIAH-2 @4K: 92.2 vs 56.2. 'Gated DeltaNet achieves the lowest overall perplexity across tasks among RNN models' (20K extrapolation). Hybrid table: H1 (GDN+SWA) 56.40, H2 (Mamba2+GDN+SWA) 56.18. Independently corroborated by third-party adoption (Qwen3-Next uses Gated DeltaNet; Kimi KDA extends it). Verifier caveats: originating-authors' own evals; real-world retrieval margin over Mamba2 narrows (30.6 vs 29.8 avg); GDN-2 confirms the original numbers while superseding the architecture.

**Sources:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06464 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22791

### 3. (confidence: medium; votes: 3-0, 3-0 (two merged claims))

MoBA (Mixture of Block Attention) is the sparse-attention pattern with quality-parity evidence at exactly the target scale: five models from 568M to 2.1B params trained to compute-optimal budgets showed validation-loss differences vs full attention within ~1e-3 at 8K context, using a simple MoE-style gate over key blocks (block size 512, top-3 selection = up to 81.25% sparsity at 8K, 95.31% at 32K) that is implementable in from-scratch PyTorch (official repo ships a mask-based 'moba_naive' reference needing only torch + flash-attn). Verdict: ablate-locally — the best-matched sparse-attention candidate for this lab, with the caveat that parity is Moonshot's own un-replicated measurement and the benefit at 4k–8k training contexts is memory/compute pattern, not proven quality gain.

**Evidence:** Paper (verbatim): 'the validation loss differences between these two attention mechanisms remain consistent within a range of 1e-3'; Table 1 lists exactly 568M/822M/1.1B/1.5B/2.1B models; 'block size to 512 and select the top-3 blocks... sparsity up to 1-512x3/8192=81.25%' and '1-512x3/32768=95.31%' at 32K. Verifier caveats: single-lab (Moonshot) result, no independent replication; the paper's own 32K trailing-token eval shows a small residual gap narrowing with scale; FlashMoBA (arXiv 2511.11571) notes the original implementation is slower than dense attention for SMALL block configs (the paper's own 512/top-3 config is fine).

**Sources:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13189 | https://github.com/MoonshotAI/MoBA

### 4. (confidence: high; votes: 3-0, 3-0, 3-0 (three merged claims))

DeepSeek NSA is quality-neutral-to-positive but its evidence does not reach the lab's scale and its wins are long-context wins: the natively-pretrained NSA model beats full attention on averages (0.456 vs 0.443 general; +0.032 LongBench; +0.075/+0.054 AIME at 8k/16k post reasoning-SFT), but the only pretraining validation is a 27B-total/3B-active MoE (30 layers, GQA 4 groups/64 heads, 72+2 experts) on 270B tokens — nothing at ≤2B dense — and the headline 11.6x decode / 9.0x forward / 6.0x backward speedups are measured at 64k context, shrinking to roughly 4x at 8k and less below. Verdict: skip for the 150M–500M proxy stage (three-branch Triton kernel complexity, benefits mostly absent at 4k–8k); track for a later long-context phase, and prefer ASA/MoBA which have sub-2B evidence.

**Evidence:** Paper (ACL 2025, verbatim): '27B total parameters with 3B active parameters... pretrained on 270B tokens of 8k-length texts'; 'up to 11.6x speedup at 64k context-length' (decode), 9.0x forward/6.0x backward; Table 4 shows expected decode speedup ~4x at 8k. Quality tables verified exactly (DROP +0.042, GSM8K +0.034, LongBench 0.469 vs 0.437, AIME 0.121 vs 0.046 @8k). Verifier caveats: originating-lab (DeepSeek) single comparison; third-party follow-ups (arXiv 2509.24663, 2511.00819) report NSA qualifications at ~1B scale — the compression/sliding branches extrapolate poorly and the sliding window can shortcut the selection branch — supporting the 'requires local ablation' conclusion.

**Sources:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11089 | https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1126.pdf

### 5. (confidence: medium; votes: 3-0, 3-0 (two merged claims; related KV-cache claim refuted 1-2))

Alternating Sparse Attention (ASA) supplies the missing sub-2B sparse-attention evidence: simply alternating layers between local (sliding-window) and global (compressed/selective) attention — instead of running all NSA branches every layer — raises NSA's S-NIAH-2 retrieval at 8k from 52.2% to 97.8% at 340M with commonsense average unchanged (43.80), and the full ASA method (alternation + MLA on the SWA branch + GLA on global branches) matches or beats both GQA full attention and NSA at 340M/15B-tokens and 1.3B/100B-tokens (commonsense avg 53.10 vs 52.96 NSA vs 51.45 GQA at 1.3B; LongBench 18.25 vs 16.78 vs 16.49). Peer-reviewed at ACL 2026 Findings. Verdict: ablate-locally — the layer-alternation idea is cheap to implement, composes naturally with the lab's planned hybrid stack, and is the top-ranked new attention pattern for a local lane.

**Evidence:** Abstract (verbatim): 'Experiments on models from 340M to 1.3B parameters (trained on 15B and 100B tokens) show our method matches or exceeds full attention and native sparse attention in both common-sense reasoning and long-context understanding tasks.' Table 1 verified: S-NIAH-2 @8k 52.2 (NSA) → 97.8 (NSA w. alt), commonsense avg 43.80 both. Verifier caveats: single lab (Renmin Univ. + Meituan), no independent replication; on hardest retrieval task S-NIAH-3 full attention still beats ASA at some context lengths (97.2/90.8 vs 83.4/62.6 at 340M); training context only 8K so 'long-context' is modest; a related claim that the latent-attention variant halves KV-cache without hurting quality was REFUTED in verification (1-2) — adopt the alternation finding, not the KV-halving one.

**Sources:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.00819 | https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.695

### 6. (confidence: high; votes: 3-0, 3-0 (two merged claims))

Multi-token prediction (MTP) should be skipped at the lab's scale despite its code-generation fame: in Meta's own scaling sweep (300M/600M/1.3B/3B/6.7B/13B, trained from scratch on ≥91B tokens of code), MTP models are WORSE than next-token baselines at 300M–1.3B — exactly the proxy and target band — with the celebrated gains (+12% HumanEval, +17% MBPP problems solved with 4-token prediction) appearing only at 13B. Verdict: skip for pretraining at 0.4B–2B; if revisited, use the ACL 2025 NTP-to-MTP curriculum result as the mitigation to test, or reserve MTP heads for a speculative-decoding role rather than a quality objective.

**Evidence:** Figure 3 caption (verbatim, ICML 2024): 'Multi-token prediction models are worse than the baseline for small model sizes, but outperform the baseline at scale.' 0.3B/0.6B deltas negative on MBPP/HumanEval pass@1/10/100; 1.3B break-even to negative. Abstract: 'Our 13B parameter models solves 12% more problems on HumanEval and 17% more on MBPP than comparable next-token models' — verified against Figures 1 and 3 (MBPP 26.0 +4.5 pass@1 ≈ +17% relative). Baselines parameter-matched. Corroboration: ACL 2025 curriculum paper (arXiv 2505.22757) independently confirms small LMs struggle with MTP and proposes an NTP→MTP curriculum as the fix; DeepSeek-V3 adopted MTP only at large scale. This is a rare anti-cherry-picked negative result reported by the method's own authors.

**Sources:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22757

### 7. (confidence: high; votes: 3-0, 3-0, 3-0 (three merged claims))

Hyper-connections (dynamic, expansion n=4) have direct in-band evidence: on OLMo-1B dense at 500B tokens they cut validation loss ~0.03 (V2 2.811→2.781, V3 2.544→2.515) with downstream avg 62.5%→63.8%, and on OLMoE-1B-7B they converge 1.8x faster with +6 ARC-Challenge — at near-zero parameter/FLOP cost (+0.033% params, +0.200% FLOPs) BUT a measured +26.1% training-memory footprint (41.11→51.86GB in the paper's setup), which the paper's 'negligible overhead' framing understates and which is material on a single 48GB GPU. Verdict: ablate-locally in mHC-constrained form (see companion finding), budgeting the memory hit or using the paper's recomputation mitigation; without headroom, deprioritize below Peri-LN and MoBA.

**Evidence:** ICLR 2025 paper (ByteDance Seed), Table 1: OLMo-1B-DHC×4 at 500B tokens, V2 loss 2.811→2.781, V3 2.544→2.515. Figure 1/Sec 4.4: OLMoE-1B-7B 'converges 1.8 times faster', ~0.027 loss reduction, +6 ARC-Challenge, +1.2 MMLU Var. Tables 7-9: +0.03349% params, +0.200% FLOPs, measured memory 41.11GB→51.86GB (+26.1%, activation-driven). Corroboration of both gains and memory cost: Frac-Connections (arXiv 2503.14125, same group) exists specifically to cut HC memory; DeepSeek mHC confirms HC-style gains at 27B while citing 'notable memory access overhead' scaling ~quadratically in n. Verifier caveats: exact numbers are the originating lab's, no independent replication of the OLMo runs; an unadjudicated OpenReview complaint alleges attribution issues but does not dispute the measurements.

**Sources:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.19606 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14125

### 8. (confidence: medium; votes: 3-0, 3-0, 3-0 (three merged claims))

If hyper-connections are adopted, use DeepSeek's mHC variant, not vanilla HC: at 27B scale vanilla HC exhibited a loss surge around step 12k with composite residual-mapping gain magnitudes peaking ~3000 (exploding residual streams); mHC fixes this by Sinkhorn-Knopp-projecting the residual mixing matrices onto doubly stochastic matrices (spectral norm ≤1, restoring the identity-mapping property, degenerating to plain residuals at n=1), reduces the gain magnitude by three orders of magnitude, and still beats both baseline and HC (final loss −0.021 vs baseline at 27B-MoE/262B tokens; +2.1% BBH, +2.3% DROP vs HC). Verdict: implement HC only with the mHC constraint (a ~20-iteration Sinkhorn projection, modest from-scratch complexity); the instability was demonstrated at 27B so it is an extrapolated caution at 1B, but the constraint is cheap insurance.

**Evidence:** Paper (DeepSeek-AI, Dec 2025, verbatim): 'HC exhibits an unexpected loss surge around the 12k step'; 'Amax Gain Magnitude yields extreme values with peaks of 3000'; 'mHC significantly reduces it by three orders of magnitude'; 'mHC utilizes the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm to entropically project H_l^res onto the Birkhoff polytope'; 'The spectral norm of a doubly stochastic matrix is bounded by 1'; 'achieving a final loss reduction of 0.021 compared to the baseline'; Table 4: BBH 43.8/48.9/51.0, DROP 47.0/51.6/53.9 (baseline/HC/mHC). Spectral-norm bound is independently verifiable math (Birkhoff-von Neumann). Verifier caveats: instability shown at 27B (original HC paper saw gains at 1B-7B without instability); 27B results are single-lab, un-replicated, though 2026 follow-ups (mHC-lite, TBP-mHC) build on the mechanism.

**Sources:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24880 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05732

### 9. (confidence: high; votes: 3-0, 3-0 (two merged claims))

Peri-LN (wrap each sublayer: y = x + Norm(Module(Norm(x)))) is the highest-value pattern not on the lab's list: in controlled 5-seed runs at 400M/1.5B/3.2B on 30B DCLM tokens it beats Pre-LN on loss AND downstream (1.5B: loss 3.18 vs 3.29, benchmark avg 56.55 vs 53.71; 3.2B: 3.11 vs 3.20, 58.56 vs 56.69), and it cuts seed-to-seed benchmark std by more than half (1.5B: ±1.22→±0.21) — directly attacking the seed-noise problem that swamps small-ablation labs. Trivial to implement (one extra RMSNorm per sublayer), used at scale by Gemma 2/3. Verdict: adopt (or at minimum first-priority ablation) — low cost, in-band evidence, and the variance reduction compounds the value of every other ablation the lab runs.

**Evidence:** ICML 2025 paper (NAVER Cloud/KAIST), Table 1 verified: 400M 3.34 vs 3.43; 1.5B 3.18/56.55 vs 3.29/53.71; 3.2B 3.11/58.56 vs 3.20/56.69 (5 seeds each). Verbatim: 'the standard deviation of the benchmark results across different training seeds is reduced by more than half with Peri-LN' — numerically ±1.63→±0.81 (400M), ±1.22→±0.21 (1.5B), ±2.32→±0.57 (3.2B). Corroboration: same pre+post sublayer norm pattern in Gemma 2/3 and OLMo 2; independent theory follow-up (arXiv 2510.09904) supports the stability mechanism. Verifier caveats: single-lab numbers; n=5 seed std is a noisy estimator; 2026 SiameseNorm paper raises LR confounds for norm comparisons generally but reports Peri-LN robust under aggressive LRs.

**Sources:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02732 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09904
